# Open Call for Pilot Projects

Turn SNOWCOP snow and ice information into practical applications for water management. Up to **four pilot projects** can receive **EUR 9,750 each** to develop and demonstrate a solution for a specific user need.

Multiple cut-offs give unsuccessful applicants an opportunity to improve and resubmit proposals after evaluation feedback. New applications can also enter a later round while the call remains open.

**Earlier cut-off rounds have priority for funding.** First-come, first-served applies across rounds. Within each round, eligible proposals meeting the quality thresholds are ranked by score; submitting earlier within the same round does not change that ranking.

**The call closes early once all four places have been allocated or the budget is fully committed.** No places or budget are reserved for later cut-offs. Remaining cut-offs will not take place after early closure, which will be announced here and through the call's communication channels. Applicants whose submissions have not yet been assessed will be informed.

## What can a pilot do?

A pilot must use an identifiable SNOWCOP dataset, method or service, address a specific user need and deliver a practical output. Examples include:

- **Hydrology:** improve basin-scale water-storage and availability assessments using SNOWCOP information alongside precipitation, evapotranspiration, streamflow or groundwater data.
- **Hydropower and reservoirs:** support river-discharge estimation, reservoir-operation decisions or hydropower planning.
- **Agriculture and water allocation:** develop or improve irrigation, agricultural or water-allocation applications.
- **Climate and meteorology:** analyse snow, ice, meltwater or drought dynamics.
- **Visualisation and decision support:** create tailored interfaces, indicators or services that help users apply SNOWCOP information.
- **Other operational applications:** demonstrate another practical use of SNOWCOP outputs for meltwater or water-resource management.

Explain how users will be involved, how usefulness will be demonstrated and how the results could continue to be used or replicated. Activities outside the scope are specified in the call guidelines.

## Who can apply?

Applicants must be legal entities established in an **EU Member State, Chile or Argentina** and be an **SME, public authority or legally established association of water managers**.

Applications must come from a **single legal entity**; consortia and joint applications are not eligible. An applicant may submit up to **two distinct pilot proposals across the whole call**. Revised versions of the same pilot do not count as additional distinct proposals.

Applicants must have the capacity to deliver the pilot, must not be SNOWCOP grant beneficiaries and must meet the other eligibility conditions in the call guidelines. SMEs must provide the required self-declaration.

## What should the application contain?

The technical proposal must contain **five sections**:

1. **Understanding of SNOWCOP:** identify the outputs you will use and their relevance to the user need.
2. **Excellence:** describe the solution, objectives, technical approach, added value and demonstration or validation method.
3. **Contribution to SNOWCOP impact:** explain user benefits, stakeholder involvement, success indicators and opportunities for continued use or replication.
4. **Implementation capacity:** present the work plan, timetable, responsibilities, milestones, outputs, resources and risks, including a concise cost justification.
5. **Team description:** describe the key people, their roles, expertise and availability.

**Maximum length: five A4 pages**, including all five sections, cost justification, figures, tables and references. Use at least 11-point text and margins of at least 2 cm. Administrative identification fields and required signed declarations are excluded only if they contain no additional technical content. Pages beyond the limit, separate CVs and additional technical annexes will not be evaluated.

A detailed itemised budget is not required. The official application form and required declarations will be added here before the call opens.

To replace a version before its cut-off, clearly withdraw the earlier version and identify the replacement in your email. Only the latest complete replacement received by that cut-off will be assessed.

## How will applications be evaluated?

After each cut-off that takes place, applications undergo an **eligibility check** followed by a **quality assessment**. The six-member evaluation panel operates under confidentiality and conflict-of-interest rules.

| Quality criterion | Score | Minimum required |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Understanding of SNOWCOP | 0–5 | 3 |
| Excellence | 0–5 | 3 |
| Contribution to SNOWCOP impact | 0–5 | 3 |
| Implementation capacity, including the team | 0–5 | 3 |

Proposals must meet every individual threshold and score **at least 15 out of 20 overall**. Team description is assessed under Implementation capacity. Qualifying proposals are ranked by total score within their round; the highest-ranked are considered for the remaining places and budget.

Ties at the funding boundary are resolved by the successive scores for impact, excellence, implementation capacity and understanding of SNOWCOP, followed, if necessary, by a documented random draw.

Applicants receive written results, consensus scores and feedback. A procedural review may be requested within ten calendar days of notification, as explained in the guidelines.

## Can an unsuccessful proposal be resubmitted?

**Yes, if a later cut-off remains available.** Use the feedback to improve the proposal and submit a complete revised application. Resubmissions are assessed afresh against the same requirements and do not retain their original submission priority. Applications are not automatically carried forward.

A proposal still under evaluation, or a pilot already selected for funding, cannot be resubmitted. The notification will indicate whether a later cut-off remains available, subject to the remaining places and budget.

## Implementation and payment

Pilots must finish **no later than 31 August 2027**. Implementation dates will be stated in the sub-grant agreement. Selection at a later cut-off does not extend the completion date; the work plan must fit the remaining eligible period.

Subject to the sub-grant agreement, 50% is paid after signature and 50% after acceptance of the final report and verification of the agreed work and outputs. The final report is due within 15 calendar days after the pilot ends and no later than 15 September 2027.
